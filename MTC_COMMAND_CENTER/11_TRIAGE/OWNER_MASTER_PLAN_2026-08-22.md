## 5. Money safety: risk buckets, sizing and the Guardian

I want strategy-level rules and account-level protection kept apart.

- **Risk buckets** — day, swing, position — each with its own view, capital budget and risk limits. **The actual numbers are not set yet**; any split proposed so far is a simulation hypothesis, not my decision.
- **Every bucket starts at 1x leverage.** Increases require evidence, and each one is a new decision by me.
- **One shared risk allocator** computes quantity, and **the same code runs in the backtest and in production**. If the allocator is built but never simulated, the design is worse than what I have now — that is the single failure I most want prevented. **So the allocator is built by the same piece of work that rebuilds the research engine (corrected 2026-08-22).** The earlier plan let the research side run against a *stand-in* allocator with a label on it, and that label was enough to pass. It should not be: a labelled substitute is not the shared allocator, and letting one through is exactly the failure above with a sticker on it. **The rebuild delivers the real allocator and uses it; a stand-in is a work-in-progress state whose results are screening only and can never count. The later runtime work wires in that same accepted allocator and proves it is the same one — it does not build a second.**
- **Who does what, in order, so nobody is doing two jobs.** *(Clarified 2026-08-22 — the earlier version was circular: it said the strategy brain knows nothing about my account, and also that it hands over an account snapshot. Made consistent later the same day — see the note below.)* **The strategy asks** — "risk this much, with the stop here" — and it **never sees my account balance and never produces a tradeable quantity**. **A separate step binds that request** to one frozen picture of the account and one named policy, and that step does no arithmetic at all. **The allocator is the only thing that turns the request into a number.** **The Guardian says yes to that exact number or says no** — and only after that yes does a real, executable quantity exist. If a limit is hit, the answer is **no**, not a quietly smaller order.

  **What "never names a quantity" does and does not mean** *(corrected 2026-08-22).* The earlier wording said the strategy never names a quantity and never names a cash amount — while the same documents offered "fixed quantity" and "fixed notional" as ways a strategy can be written. Both cannot be true. **The distinction is where the number comes from.** A strategy may carry a **fixed number written into its own frozen settings** — "half a unit", "five hundred dollars' worth", "one percent risk" — because that is just part of the strategy, the same as its stop rule. It is a **request**, and the allocator is free to refuse it. What the strategy may **never** do is produce a number that came from **looking at my account**, or hand over anything that counts as an approved or executable order size. **It asks; it does not decide.** The same rule fixes a second muddle: **the risk-per-trade figure in a request comes from the strategy's own frozen settings, not from the allocation policy.** The allocation policy sets the **ceilings**; the allocator checks the request against them and either passes the full thing along or refuses it. And if a strategy's original source describes sizing in a way that cannot be written down without peeking at my account, it is **recorded as not expressible** and given a named standard substitute — it never gets solved by letting the strategy look.
- **The Portfolio Guardian only authorizes or rejects.** It never silently resizes an order. Every rejection carries a readable reason.
- **Stopping the system is four different things, and they stay four different things.** *(Added 2026-08-22; sharpened 2026-08-22 after a second look.)* **The V2 target is:** **DISARM** stops new trades being opened and leaves everything else alone. **KILL** cancels the orders that would **open or add to** a position and latches the system off — **it does not close my positions, and it deliberately leaves my protective stop sitting at the exchange.** That last part matters: a "kill" that also swept away my stop would leave a live position with nothing behind it, which is worse than not pressing the button. So the check after a V2 KILL is not *"is everything gone"* — it is *"are the entry orders gone **and** is my protection still there"*. **FLATTEN** closes positions, and it is a separate button with its own confirmation and its own second factor; **because closing a position sometimes means moving the protective order out of the way first, that job belongs to FLATTEN and to nothing else.** And a fourth thing — the system automatically reducing a position on its own — **is not built, is not named anywhere on the live screen, and is not authorized.** The reason to write this down is that "kill" and "flatten" were being used as one word, and under pressure that is exactly how somebody closes a book when they meant to stop the machine. **One honest note about today, and it has two halves that must not be run together** *(separated 2026-08-24, map #123 fold; **the first half corrected 2026-08-24, G1 final repair before audit round 3, because it was simply wrong about my own code — see the correction note below**).* **What the code in this repository says:** there is **one kill command with an optional "and flatten" switch, and no separate flatten command.** That part is true and stands. What that one command actually does depends on which version of the safety database it is running against. **On an older database version it only latches the system off — it cancels nothing and closes nothing.** **On the newer version, which is built and tested, it already does the careful thing: it cancels the orders that would open or add to a position, and if I have a position open and did not ask for a flatten, it insists on finding my protective stop still sitting there and leaves it alone — and refuses to proceed if that protection is missing.** **So my code does not blindly sweep everything away.** **What it does not have is a separate, properly confirmed FLATTEN button of its own** — its own command, its own confirmation, its own record — **and that, along with the whole operator-facing separation and the proof that the right version is switched on, is what the V2 plan still has to build.**

  **Correction note, 2026-08-24 (G1 final repair before audit round 3).** This paragraph used to say the kill path *"calls `cancel_all()`, so as written it cancels all working orders indiscriminately"*, and that at source level V1 provides **neither** protection-preserving KILL semantics **nor** a separate FLATTEN operation. **The first part is not true of my code**: the kill path does not call `cancel_all()` at all, and under the newer database version it already cancels selectively and already preserves my protection. **The line references these documents were quoting were pointing at the wrong part of the file entirely.** **The second part was half right and is now stated exactly:** the separate FLATTEN operation genuinely does not exist, and building it is real V2 work. **I would rather have my own system described accurately than described as worse than it is** — a plan that tells me to build something I already have is as wasteful as one that tells me I have something I do not. **Nothing about the V2 work above is reduced by this correction, no decision of mine changed, and nothing here was tested, run or deployed.**

  **What the running process on my VPS actually does is a separate question, and nobody has answered it.** The repository's own records say the deployed database is on schema **v4** — the older version — with the later safety mechanisms built and tested but switched off; and if that record is right, the deployed "kill and flatten" would only latch the system off and block new submissions, cancelling and flattening nothing. **But that is a record, not an observation. Nobody contacted the host, and nobody was allowed to.** The deployed version, configuration and schema version are **UNVERIFIED**, so **I do not treat any of it as established current truth** — including which of the two behaviours above is the one actually running. It gets verified under the host-contact gate, or it stays **UNKNOWN**. **Nothing in the V2 semantics above is deployed anywhere, and nothing here authorizes contacting the host to find out.**
- **The way I stop it must not have a single point of failure.** *(Added 2026-08-22.)* At least **two separately registered security keys**, not both on the same device, each proven to work on its own — and a **written, rehearsed way to close positions directly at the exchange** when my own software cannot be reached at all, timed against the five-minute target my own live gate already sets. The stop order sitting at the exchange helps if the process dies, but it protects one position at one price; it does not close a book and it does not answer to me. **This gets drilled on testnet, with the times written down.** *(Draft-value note, 2026-08-24, map #123 fold — my wording above and D-16's acknowledged text are unchanged. The **five minutes** comes from my live-gate document, which is still an unsigned draft, so that figure is an **unratified draft proposal and stays `[OPEN]`** until I ratify it. **The obligation stands either way:** a stated flatten-time target must exist and the out-of-band path must be timed against it, with the elapsed time recorded. What is open is the value, not the duty, and no open value counts as met by default.)*
- **What "no resizing" does and does not mean.** It is a rule about **sizing a new order**: the Guardian may not quietly hand the exchange a different quantity from the one the allocator proposed. It is **not** a ban on ever reducing or closing a position I already hold in an emergency. If such a thing is ever built, it is **its own explicit, separately authorized, tested safety policy with a visible reason** — never a silent resize. **Writing this down authorizes neither building it nor using it**, and the detailed design belongs to later work.
- **The stop is a real reduce-only order at the exchange**, so protection survives the trading process being killed — and the backtest models it the same way.
- **Fail closed.** If the account picture the system sized against is stale or inconsistent, nothing is submitted.
- **No false diversification.** Family, overlap and correlation caps, applied at promotion and at runtime — and simulated, not just enforced live. A breach rejects; it never quietly trims.

## 6. How a strategy earns its way to real money

One connected ladder, walked in order, with no shortcuts:

**Discovery → source extraction → testing → statistical validation → freeze → forward shadow → paper/testnet → portfolio test → limited live → scale, suspend or retire.**

The rules I care about most:

- **Test the original source rules separately from my enriched version**, so I can see where the edge actually comes from.
- **Missing rules get written down, not invented.** They may be completed only from a small standard catalogue, and the gaps travel with the candidate in a ledger.
- **A missing stop or target does not automatically block forward observation** — but it does block live capital until the substitute has evidence.
- **Freeze before the forward clock starts.** Any code, parameter or module change creates a new package identity and a new evidence period. No exceptions, because that is the only thing that makes forward evidence mean anything. **And freezing the strategy alone was not enough (corrected 2026-08-22):** the risk allocator, the allocation policy, the Guardian's thresholds, the bucket and account limits, the freshness settings, the stop behaviour, the exchange adapter and the fee model could all change underneath an accumulating clock without anything noticing. **All of them are now part of the identity the evidence belongs to, and changing any of them resets the clock** — the old window is kept as history and never counted toward a gate. **Said plainly, because some parts of the documents still had it the old way: freezing the strategy fixes the strategy. The clock does not start there. The clock starts when the *whole* setup is fixed — strategy plus allocator plus policies plus stop behaviour plus adapter plus fees — and that whole setup has been admitted to run in that environment. Change any part of it and that environment's clock goes back to zero.**
- **Forward shadow starts early and runs in parallel** with the slower statistical work. Two constraints bind at once — finding a statistically credible candidate, and the passage of calendar time — and neither can substitute for the other.
- **Watching one version must not secretly shape another.** *(Added 2026-08-22.)* Freezing stops me from editing a strategy while I watch it, but it does not stop me from watching one version and then using what I saw to pick the settings of its sibling. Related versions are now **recorded as related**, every look at forward data goes into an **append-only log**, and the "nobody looked at this" window is **calculated from that log rather than claimed**. **If the log is missing or incomplete, the answer is zero clean evidence, not the benefit of the doubt.** This has to be in place **before any strategy is ever declared a live candidate** — it was previously written down as a known hole and left for later.
- **Many candidates into shadow; far fewer onto exchange testnet**, and the testnet fleet size is set by measured exchange limits and reconciliation reliability, not by a number someone picked.
- **Paper and testnet are two different things, and my own gate already treats them as two.** *(Named 2026-08-22.)* My live-trading gate has a **paper soak** condition and a **testnet proof** condition, and they are separate items with no partial credit. Until now only the testnet side had anybody's name against it, so in practice the testnet work was the only thing the plan could have offered for both — which is exactly the swap I said I did not want. **The same existing piece of work now carries both, deliberately kept apart.** The paper side runs on **my own machine with simulated fills**: a plan written down and fixed **before** the clock starts, an **immutable start date**, **at least eight to sixteen weeks**, **at least thirty new trades taken in that window**, **not one unexplained reconciliation break**, and **if it is stopped, it does not resume — a new plan gets approved and the count starts again.** The testnet side stays what it already was: the real order lifecycle, the native stop, killing the process for real, the break-glass drills and the capacity measurement. **Separate clocks, separate counts, separate records, separate claims. A testnet week is not a paper week and a testnet trade is not one of the thirty. Neither one covers for the other.** **No new requirement, no new safeguard and no new package was created to say this, and neither of them is being run.** *(Draft-value note, 2026-08-24, map #123 fold — my wording above is unchanged. The **eight to sixteen weeks** and **at least thirty new trades** figures are the numbers written in my live-gate document, and that document is still an unsigned draft, so both are **unratified draft proposals and stay `[OPEN]`** until I ratify them — as do the other unratified figures in it: the lockbox trade count and `robust_final` value, the signal-agreement percentage, the flatten-time target and precondition 10's capital number. **The requirements do not weaken:** a plan fixed before the clock starts, an immutable start date, a minimum window, a minimum count of new trades on that environment alone, zero unexplained reconciliation breaks, and no restart without a newly approved plan all remain required, as do the fourteen preconditions and the no-partial-credit rule. What is open is the value, never the obligation. No number is invented, lowered or raised.)*
- **Getting into shadow or testnet is its own recorded decision.** *(Added 2026-08-22.)* Before anything is loaded, a strategy passes **objective checks** — does it replay identically, does it read the future, does it repaint on a closed bar, is the data clean, does it fall over on basics — and the result is a **permanent, written admission** naming exactly which environment it may run in. **If nothing has been admitted, nothing loads.** This is deliberately **not** the same as approving it for real money, which stays with the promotion screen. *(Why: the old rules said only the promotion screen could admit anything, but the promotion screen arrives two phases after shadow and testnet need to run — so either nothing could ever start, or somebody would quietly break the rule.)*
- **Promotion produces a permanent decision record**, created on its own screen, separate from the explorer. Execution can load **only** frozen packages traceable to a record from the right authority for the environment being asked for.
- **A research number only counts if it came out of the real simulator.** *(Added 2026-08-22.)* The engine that currently produces my promotion numbers models almost none of what the live system actually enforces. Rebuilding it on the one strategy brain and the one risk allocator is now its own piece of work, and **until it lands, results from the old engine are screening only and cannot promote anything.** Anything switched on is either simulated or **listed by name as not simulated**, and if something important is on that list, **the strategy is blocked.**
- **First live deployment is deliberately tiny**: one strategy, at most 1% of the account, behind a gate I sign myself. **To be exact: the 1% is the maximum capital allocated to that strategy. How much a stop-out is allowed to cost me is a separate and lower limit, and it must be defined and evidenced before I authorize anything live. No number is invented for it here.** **And to be exact about the gate itself (corrected 2026-08-22): it has FOURTEEN hard conditions, not six as these documents previously said. All fourteen are required, there is no partial credit, and they are evidenced together in one dated decision against the exact configuration that will trade. The two caps above sit alongside those fourteen — they replace none of them, and the hard capital number still needs my signature separately.**

## 7. Research I can look at

- **One compact record per trial** — parameters, results, rejection reasons — written in a format that does not depend on which optimizer produced it. Full artifacts kept for selected candidates; the rest reproducible on demand.
